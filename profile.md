## Diagnosis — supersedes the earlier one

Apply the three-way test:

**Demand problem?** YES, and it is the primary constraint. 5 inquiries per month
is very low. At 20% conversion that ceiling is about 1 new client per month
regardless of how well intake performs.

**Intake-conversion problem?** Secondary. 20% has room to improve, but doubling
it against 5 inquiries adds only 1 client per month. Median 24 hours to first
shift indicates intake speed is already good.

**Staffing-capacity problem?** MATERIAL, not primary. 3 declines against roughly
15 inquiries in 90 days means about 20% of demand is being refused. Any
increase in inquiries will hit this ceiling.

### What this means for the agent

The earlier portfolio item, "Intake conversion SLA and follow-up operating
system," was built on incorrect figures (22 inquiries, 9% conversion, 2 staffing
declines). Those numbers are wrong. Re-diagnose from the figures above.

Ranked by likely return per unit of my time:

1. **Referral relationship development.** Three of the highest-intent channels
   in home care are at literally zero: SNF and rehab case managers, elder law
   attorneys, and geriatric care managers. These cost time, not money, and
   produce longer-tenure, higher-hour clients than online leads.
2. **Google reviews and local presence.** Zero reviews against 5 competitors
   makes the $2,000/month online spend structurally unproductive. This is a
   process problem, not a budget problem.
3. **Staffing capacity.** 20% of inquiries refused. Fixing demand without
   fixing this converts new inquiries into new declines.
4. **Intake conversion.** Real but small at current volume. Revisit once
   inquiries exceed roughly 15 per month.
5. **More paid advertising.** Last. $2,000/month is already producing 1 client.
