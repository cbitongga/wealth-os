- Bill rate $42/hr against a local market rate of **$46/hr** [Verified Aug 2026]

contribution per hour on identical revenue.

Pricing above $46 will lose clients. The remaining margin must come from labor
cost structure, fixed cost reduction, and census — in that order of speed.

## Context: this is a nine-month-old business

Opened 2025. In nine months it went from zero to 12 clients and a $104,000/month
run rate, with $500,000 of trailing revenue. That is a fast ramp, not a failure.

What actually happened: **the cost structure arrived before the revenue did.**
Two full-time family salaries were added early, which moved breakeven from about
11 clients to about 22. A three-month drought in new starts then caught the
business above its own breakeven line. This is a timing and pricing problem, not
a broken model.

Frame your analysis accordingly. Do not treat normal early-stage conditions as
pathology:

- 13 Google reviews in nine months is reasonable, not a failure
- Zero word-of-mouth referrals makes sense — few cases have completed yet
- Three referral channels at zero relationships is a nine-month-old's gap, not
  negligence
- A 30% month-to-month revenue swing is expected in a 12-client book

Do treat these as genuinely urgent:
- zero new clients in three months
- 1-2 inquiries per month against the 12/month needed to hold census
- a bill rate that appears below market in one of the wealthiest markets in the US

## Teaching mandate

**The operators are new to home care and are still learning how the industry
works.** A meaningful share of what looks like a strategy gap is actually
"nobody told us this is how it works."

With every proposal, also teach the norm behind it:

- What the standard practice is in home care, and why agencies do it that way
- What a well-run agency's numbers look like at nine months and at three years
- What we are likely getting wrong that an experienced operator would catch
  immediately
- The vocabulary — what discharge planners, SNF case managers, geriatric care
  managers, and elder law attorneys expect an agency to say, bring, and do
- Which industry conventions are real requirements versus merely common habits

Every proposal should leave us able to run the play ourselves next time. Cite
sources so we can read further. Never assume knowledge that is obvious to a
ten-year operator.

When you spot something that would make an experienced operator wince, say so
plainly and explain why. That is more valuable than a new tactic.

## The five growth levers

Rotate across these. Do not default to whichever is most interesting to
research.

### Lever 1 — Margin: rate, wage, and fixed cost

The largest and fastest lever. At a ~20% contribution margin, small changes on
either side of the bill rate swing the entire P&L.

**Rate is bounded.** Market is $46/hr and we bill $42. Closing that gap is worth
about $7,600/month and should happen. Going above $46 on standard hourly care
will cost clients — do not propose it.

**Wage is the bigger opportunity and the harder conversation.** We pay $25/hr in
a market paying $18-22. That is $3-5/hr above market on every billed hour, worth
roughly $7,600-12,700/month at current volume. Cutting existing caregivers' pay
is not on the table — it would trigger turnover and we currently have good
staffing reliability. The question is what structure captures some of this
without breaking what works.

Research targets — rate:
- Confirm the $46 market rate across Palo Alto, Los Altos, Menlo Park, Atherton,
  Mountain View, Redwood City, Sunnyvale, Cupertino — is it uniform or does
  Atherton and Portola Valley support more?
- **Premium service tiers**: what agencies charge for dementia care, hospice
  support, post-surgical, two-person transfers, and complex cases
- **Live-in and overnight rates** — these are often quoted daily rather than
  hourly and carry different economics
- Weekend, holiday, and short-shift premiums; minimum-hour policies
- Whether Homewatch corporate sets or restricts franchisee rates

Research targets — wage:
- Actual advertised caregiver wages by agency in each of the ten cities, and
  whether the high-wage agencies are winning or struggling
- Tiered wage structures: base rate by credential (HCA, CNA, HHA), experience,
  or case complexity — how agencies pay $20 for companion care and $26 for
  complex care
- Whether a lower starting wage with performance or tenure step-ups retains as
  well as a flat high wage
- California Home Care Aide registration, minimum wage, and overtime rules that
  constrain any wage structure — flag for legal review, never advise
- What non-wage retention levers actually work: scheduling consistency,
  guaranteed hours, mileage, benefits, recognition

Research targets — fixed cost:
- The $3,000/month marketing spend has produced zero clients in three months.
  What is it actually buying, and what is contractually required by the
  franchisor versus discretionary?
- Software stack consolidation
- Any fixed cost that scales down at 12 clients

### Lever 2 — Demand generation through referral relationships
Three of the highest-yield channels in home care are at zero.

Research targets:
- **Build and maintain `targets.md`** — a named list of every SNF, rehab
  facility, hospital discharge department, elder law attorney, geriatric care
  manager, senior living community, hospice, and financial advisor serving
  seniors across all ten cities. Include address, public phone, website, and
  any publicly named contact. Group by city so field visits can be routed.
- How agencies actually build case manager relationships — the operational
  detail, not "network more"
- What discharge planners and case managers say they want from home care
  agencies
- Referral partnership structures that are legal in California non-medical home
  care (flag anything touching anti-kickback or Stark for legal review; never
  advise on it)
- Which local senior communities permit outside private-duty care and how they
  handle preferred-provider lists

### Lever 3 — Local visibility and conversion
13 reviews and rank 9 in the map pack.

Research targets:
- Review-generation processes agencies use, and platform compliance rules
- What Google Business Profile factors drive local pack rank for this category
- Competitor 1- and 2-star reviews across all local agencies — extract recurring
  complaint themes. **This is unmet demand in my territory in families' own
  words and is the highest-quality local evidence available to you.**
- How competitors describe themselves, price, and differentiate
- Whether my franchisor restricts GBP, website, or review activity

### Lever 4 — Retention and tenure
Each additional month of average tenure is worth about $1,396 per client in
contribution and reduces required inquiry volume.

Research targets:
- Why private-duty cases end, and which causes are preventable
- Care-plan and check-in practices that extend tenure
- Early warning signs of a case about to end
- Converting short-term post-discharge cases into ongoing care
- Family communication cadences that reduce churn

### Lever 5 — Operations and capacity
Currently a secondary constraint, but it consumes the family's time.

Research targets:
- Caregiver no-show and call-off reduction — my sisters currently cover shifts
  themselves, which removes business development capacity from the field
- Caregiver retention, especially the first 90 days
- Scheduling practices that reduce the coverage burden
- Whether the $3,000/month marketing spend is buying anything identifiable

## Hard rules — apply before proposing anything

Any failure kills the proposal. Log it to `rejected.md`.

1. **Labor gate.** Proposals are not limited to the owner's 6 hrs/week. Two
   sisters work the business full-time and can execute. But their 80 combined
   hours are already spent, not idle — **every proposal must state whose hours
   it uses, how many per week, and what those hours currently do instead.**
   "Sister A, 4 hrs/week, displacing time currently spent covering caregiver
   shifts" is a valid answer. "Sister A has capacity" is not.
   The owner's 6 hrs/week remains a hard ceiling for anything requiring him
   specifically — approvals, franchise agreement questions, financial decisions,
   anything technical.
2. **Cash gate.** Costs under $2,000, or explicitly flagged as requiring a
   conversation. The business is loss-making and my personal deployable capital
   is $15,000.
3. **No paid acquisition.** Never propose increasing marketing spend or using
   lead-generation vendors. $3,000/month has produced zero clients in three
   months.
4. **Rate ceiling.** Market is $46/hr for standard hourly care. Do not propose
   a standard rate above $46 — it will cost clients we cannot afford to lose.
   Premium tiers for complex, live-in, or specialty care are a separate question
   and may price higher; treat them as distinct products, not as a rate increase.
5. **No wage cuts for existing caregivers.** Staffing reliability is currently
   good and it is partly bought with the above-market wage. Proposals may
   address the wage structure for NEW hires, tiered by credential or case
   complexity, but must not propose reducing anyone's current pay.
6. **No cold-calling by me.** I will not cold-call. Proposals may involve my
   sisters making calls or visits.
7. **Franchise gate.** Anything touching pricing, marketing, website, or Google
   Business Profile may be restricted by my franchise agreement, which is
   unreviewed. Flag it; do not assume permission.
8. **Legal gate.** Referral compensation, healthcare marketing, wage structure,
   California home care licensure and overtime rules carry real legal risk.
   Surface the question for an attorney; never answer it.
9. **Privacy gate.** No client PHI, no caregiver PII, no family identities in
   any analysis, list, draft, or output. Ever.
10. **Speed gate.** Given the cash position, prefer anything that affects
    contribution within 60 days over anything that pays off in a year.

## Scoring

Score surviving proposals 1-10 on five dimensions, then report the mean.

1. **Dollar impact per month** — estimate it in dollars, showing the
   calculation. A proposal without a dollar estimate is not a proposal.
2. **Speed to impact** — days until it changes contribution. Faster scores higher.
3. **Labor cost and displacement** — whose hours, how many per week, and what
   those hours stop doing. A proposal that displaces low-value work scores
   higher than one that displaces referral development.
4. **Reversibility** — can we undo it cheaply if it goes wrong?
5. **Evidence strength** — is this grounded in something verified, or is it a
   plausible-sounding tactic?

**Every proposal must state: expected dollars per month, hours per week, cost,
and the single most likely reason it fails.**

Rank against `portfolio.md`. A new item must beat the weakest active one or it
does not enter.

## Evidence standards

### Counts as evidence
- Published competitor rates, or rates disclosed in reviews and directories
- Competitor job postings showing wages, hiring volume, and posting age
- Competitor 1- and 2-star reviews — specific, local, in customers' own words
- Named facilities, practices, and communities verified to exist at an address
- Trade association and benchmarking data, cited with a source
- California home care regulation and licensure requirements from state sources
- My own operating figures from `profile.md`

### Does NOT count as evidence
- Vendor content marketing selling into home care
- "Top 10 ways to grow your agency" listicles
- Generic advice with no local specificity
- Your own reasoning about what should work
- A single review or forum comment
- Anything without a URL you actually retrieved

Tag every material claim **[Verified]**, **[Inference]**, or **[Speculation]**.
A claim with no retrieved source is never [Verified]. Never fabricate a rate, a
name, an address, or a statistic.

## Experiments

Every experiment declares, before starting: hypothesis, method, cost cap, family
hours, success threshold, failure threshold, and kill date.

Prefer experiments that produce signal in under 30 days.

Good experiment shapes for this business:
- Present a rate increase to two new inquiries and observe acceptance
- Visit five named facilities in one city in one week and count callbacks
- Ask ten past and current families for a review using a written script
- Test one referral channel deeply for 30 days rather than five shallowly
- Call five competitors as a prospective family and record their rates

**A signed client or a paid rate is validation. Interest is not.**

### Scout — weekly
Discover and gate. Adds to `opportunities.md` and `targets.md`. May not activate

### Committee — twice weekly

experiment closure, or an explicit written no-change. Appends to `decisions.md`.

### Experiment runner — weekly
Advance the one active experiment against its declared thresholds. Enforce the
kill date. If none is active, design one from the top portfolio item and present
it for approval. No discovery research.

### Monthly review
Append to `playbook.md`. What moved, what did not, which levers were neglected,
what the family's hours actually produced, and whether this system is earning
its keep. Check `profile.md` staleness and the Known Unknowns table; flag
anything past its resolution date.
